## Design Context

### Users
- **Primary audiences**: (1) Families in crisis — recently discharged from hospital, urgent timeline, high anxiety, need trustworthy guidance fast; and (2) Planners researching ahead — deliberate, methodical, comparing options weeks or months in advance.
- **Context**: Users are making one of the most consequential care decisions for a loved one. They may be grieving, stressed, or time-pressured. The interface must deliver clarity without adding cognitive load.
- **Job to be done**: Quickly understand whether a nursing home is safe and adequately staffed, compare alternatives, and feel confident in the data behind the recommendation.

### Brand Personality
- **Three words**: Independent. Trustworthy. Urgent.
- **Voice**: Direct, factual, no fluff. Speaks like an investigative journalist, not a marketer. Calls out conflicts of interest plainly. Uses specifics (percentages, dollar amounts, data sources) to build credibility.
- **Tone**: Serious but not cold. Urgent but not alarmist. Every word should earn the user's trust.
- **Emotional goals**: Confidence that the data is unbiased, urgency to act on poor grades, calm clarity when comparing options.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Editorial / newspaper — serious, credible, unadorned. Think ProPublica or The Marshall Project, not A Place for Mom or CMS.gov.
- **References**: Investigative journalism sites (clean grids, strong typography, limited palette, data-forward). The Playfair Display (headings) + Inter (UI, numerals) pairing on a cool off-white background carries that tone.
- **Anti-references**: 
  - **Referral sites** (A Place for Mom, etc.): stock photos of smiling seniors, soft pastels, overly friendly copy, hidden incentives.
  - **Government sites** (CMS): cluttered tables, outdated UI, overwhelming data dumps, no visual hierarchy.
- **Theme**: Light mode only. Cool off-white (`#F7F9FA`) background, navy ink (`#0B1D33`), and a teal brand accent (`#16897A` for fills and marks, `#0F6E62` for links and small text). Alarm is expressed through the grade scale (`--alarm` = grade F ink), not a separate accent color. There is no burgundy.
- **Design system**: tokens, logo rules, contrast table, grade colors and the component inventory live in `docs/brand/README.md`; the code is `src/styles/tokens.ts` and `src/templates/components.ts`.

### Design Principles
1. **Data first, decoration never** — Every visual element must serve comprehension. No stock imagery, no ornamental graphics, no decorative flourishes.
2. **Trust through transparency** — Show sources, methodology, and conflicts openly. The design should reinforce that nothing is hidden.
3. **Urgency without panic** — Use grade colors and the alarm red sparingly to highlight danger, but maintain enough calm whitespace that users can think clearly.
4. **Accessible by default** — Strong contrast, readable type sizes, clear focus states, semantic HTML. No specific WCAG mandate, but best practices are non-negotiable.
5. **Printable and shareable** — Facility pages should print cleanly (hide chrome, preserve data). Users share these pages with family and care teams.
